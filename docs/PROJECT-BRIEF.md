# Project Brief

## Working product name

FollowPilot

This is the official working name for the project. It describes helping people confidently complete post-meeting follow-up work. The name may change later through branding or market validation.

## One-sentence idea

FollowPilot helps customer-facing teams confidently complete post-meeting work without missing anything important.

## Problem

Customer-facing and internal teams spend a lot of time in meetings but often do not have time to write transcripts, capture action items, update the CRM, or create summaries in teammates' own languages. The work becomes repetitive, spread across several tools, easy to forget, and hard to evaluate. Teams also need AI-made changes to show supporting evidence.

**Fact stated by us:** These meeting follow-up tasks currently feel repetitive, scattered, easy to forget, and difficult to evaluate.

**Assumption to test:** Teams would trust and use AI help if it showed evidence for its suggested changes.

## Who has this problem

Both customer-facing and internal teams experience this problem. However, the first version of FollowPilot is intentionally focused on customer-facing teams because their post-meeting work has more immediate customer and revenue impact. Internal teams are considered a future expansion.

## Current workaround

After a meeting, teams usually use a fragmented, manual process. They reopen the recording or transcript, read notes, and try to remember important decisions. They then manually identify action items and update the places where that information belongs.

This can include updating a CRM such as HubSpot or Salesforce; writing follow-up emails in Gmail or Outlook; sending messages in Slack or Microsoft Teams; creating work in Jira, Asana, ClickUp, Linear, or Trello; and updating internal documentation in Notion or Confluence. Meeting recordings or transcripts may come from Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Fathom, Fireflies, or similar tools.

**Fact stated by us:** The follow-up work involves moving information between multiple applications and manually checking it to avoid mistakes.

**Assumption to test:** This workflow is common enough across the target teams to be a major, repeated pain.

## Why current solutions are not enough

The biggest problem is that the process is too easy to get wrong. Important information is spread across transcripts, notes, CRM records, emails, and task-management tools. People manually move information between them, so they can forget commitments, update the wrong CRM fields, assign the wrong action items, or send incomplete follow-up emails.

Saving time matters, but the main need is preventing costly mistakes and giving people confidence that nothing important was missed.

**Fact stated by us:** Manual handoffs between several tools can lead to missed or incorrect information.

**Assumption to test:** More meetings in one day directly lead to a higher chance of these mistakes.

## Proposed solution

Help people confidently complete post-meeting work without missing anything important.

The product should prepare, organize, and recommend post-meeting actions, while the person makes the final decision about anything that affects customers, CRM records, or business commitments. This keeps people in control while aiming to save them time and reduce costly mistakes.

**Assumption to test:** A single product can meaningfully reduce missed information across the different post-meeting tasks people do today.

**Assumption to test:** Human approval will create enough trust for teams to use the product regularly.

## Main user outcome

After finishing meeting follow-up, a person should be confident that nothing important was missed, every customer commitment was captured correctly, necessary follow-up actions are ready, and the information is accurate enough to trust.

They should be able to move on to their next meeting without worrying that they forgot a promise, changed the wrong information, or need to revisit the meeting later.

**Desired outcome:** Confidence in complete and accurate meeting follow-up.

**Assumption to test:** Users will consider this confidence valuable enough to change their current process.

## Riskiest assumptions

People will trust AI to prepare important post-meeting work and will prefer reviewing AI-generated suggestions over doing all the work manually.

If reviewing the AI output takes as much effort as doing the work from scratch, the product loses most of its value.

## Evidence we currently have

The strongest evidence is the product designer's repeated experience working with clients. After meetings, they review recordings, transcripts, notes, emails, and project documents to understand decisions correctly before taking action.

They have also observed that meeting assistants such as Fathom, Fireflies, Otter, tl;dv, Avoma, and Fellow mainly capture and summarize meetings. In their experience, much of the remaining follow-up work—reviewing information, updating systems, creating tasks, and sending follow-up communication—still requires manual effort.

**Evidence type:** Direct personal experience and observation of existing products; not formal user research.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet known whether customer-facing teams will genuinely prefer reviewing AI-prepared follow-up work over completing it manually.

The biggest unanswered question is how much review effort users consider acceptable before they stop trusting or using the product. The product must save enough time while still leaving users confident in the result.

## MVP boundary

The first version will focus on customer-facing teams, where missed or miscommunicated meeting information can quickly lead to poor customer experiences, incorrect CRM records, missed commitments, delayed follow-ups, and lost revenue.

This group repeats similar post-meeting workflows, making a focused solution easier to design, test, and validate. Internal teams are outside the first version and may be considered later.

## Success criteria

Users can complete their post-meeting review significantly faster while still feeling confident that nothing important was missed.

Users choose to use FollowPilot again after future meetings instead of returning to their previous manual workflow.

**Measurement still to define:** What counts as “significantly faster” and how repeat use will be measured.
